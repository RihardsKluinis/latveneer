Production process

From raw logs to sorted veneer.

Logs are peeled on two spindleless lines, at natural moisture levels without presoaking. Thickness and quality are monitored regularly.

Fresh veneer rests for at least 48 hours, allowing natural redistribution of moisture.

A 32 m, four-belt dryer powered by recycled woodchips brings veneer to the required moisture levels.

Hourly inspections ensure the ideal 4–8% moisture range is maintained.

Each sheet is manually inspected and sorted into grade packs for consistent quality.

Good to know

Veneer, in plain words.

What is spindleless (rotary) peeling?

The log is spun against a long knife so the wood peels off as one continuous sheet — like unrolling paper. A spindleless lathe grips the log with drive rollers instead of centre spindles, so it can peel down to a very small core, turning more of each birch log into usable veneer.

Rotary-cut vs sliced veneer — what's the difference?

Rotary-cut veneer is peeled in a continuous sheet around the log, giving a broad grain and full-size sheets — the standard for plywood and moulded-plywood parts. Sliced veneer is cut leaf by leaf from a flitch for decorative face grain. We produce rotary-cut core veneer.

What does FSC®-certified veneer mean?

It means the wood is tracked from responsibly managed forests through every step of production under a chain-of-custody certificate. We run both FSC® Percentage and Transfer systems and trace each pallet individually, so any order can carry the FSC® label it needs.
